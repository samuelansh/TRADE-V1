# Anti-Overengineering / Anti-Cowardice Skill

## Mission
Robustness is not the same as inactivity.

Do not confuse:
- complexity with intelligence
- more filters with better risk management
- fewer trades with higher quality
- lower drawdown with better strategy
- higher Sharpe with stronger edge
- more validation gates with more truth

## Filter budget
Every filter must document:
- purpose
- hypothesis
- evidence
- redundancy
- complexity cost
- trades removed
- opportunity cost
- out-of-sample impact

## Ablation
Compare baseline vs filtered strategy using identical unseen conditions.

Evaluate both:
- protection gained
- opportunity lost

## Filter dominance
Track which filters reject most signals. Investigate dominant filters aggressively.

## Too-safe test
Warning signs:
- extremely low activity
- very high rejection rate
- most signals rejected by secondary filters
- strategy only trades under unusually perfect conditions
- performance concentrated in very few trades

## Too-active test
Also investigate:
- excessive turnover
- tiny expected edge
- repeated entries
- noise trading
- cost sensitivity

## Anti-chicken rule
Separate:
**uncertainty requiring no trade** (stale data, broken execution, invalid risk)
from:
**normal market uncertainty** (imperfect signals, ordinary volatility).

Do not require every variable to agree perfectly before making a probabilistic decision.

## Simplicity challenge
After a promising strategy is found, deliberately remove components and retest.

If the simpler system performs similarly out of sample:
**prefer the simpler system.**

## Output
Return:
- filters evaluated
- ablation results
- opportunity cost
- complexity score
- recommended removals
