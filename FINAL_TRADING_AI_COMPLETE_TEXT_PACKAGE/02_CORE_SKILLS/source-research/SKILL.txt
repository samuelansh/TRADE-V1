# Source Research Skill

## Mission
Find reliable evidence before committing to important market, quantitative, architecture, or tooling decisions.

## Source hierarchy
Prefer, when applicable:
1. Primary academic papers and official documentation
2. Regulators, exchanges, broker documentation, standards
3. Reputable institutional research
4. High-quality secondary analysis
5. Community discussion only for practical signals or user experience

Never treat a search-result snippet as sufficient evidence.

## Research procedure
- Define the exact question.
- Search multiple independent sources when the claim matters.
- Record publication date and context.
- Separate source-derived facts from inference.
- Look actively for contradictory evidence.
- Check whether the source's methodology actually supports the conclusion.
- Record URLs/source identifiers in the research ledger.

## Anti-confirmation-bias rule
Do not search only for evidence supporting the current strategy. Deliberately search:
- criticism
- failed studies
- alternative explanations
- known limitations
- market-regime exceptions

## Update rule
When new evidence materially changes a conclusion:
- preserve the old conclusion
- explain what changed
- cite the new evidence
- update the project knowledge
- do not silently rewrite history

## Output
Return:
- Research question
- Sources
- Key findings
- Contradicting evidence
- Confidence
- What remains unknown
- Decision impact
